# Show Format

How a Control Alt Defeat episode runs, from the shoot to what ends up on screen.

## The setup

- Two hosts at a table: Aaron Fowler and Blaine McIntosh.
- Two laptops, one AI each. Each laptop lid carries that episode's team name (example: MJ / LEBRON).
- Default seating: Claude on the left, ChatGPT on the right. Guest AIs take a side as assigned.
- Each host is usually "in the corner" of one AI (Ep 1: Aaron = Team Claude, Blaine = Team ChatGPT). Sometimes a host reads their AI's line out loud, so lines must sound good in a human voice too.

## Beat by beat

1. **Intro.** "I'm Aaron." "I'm Blaine." "And this is Ctrl Alt Defeat."
2. **Topic wheel.** A wheel spins and lands on a matchup. A host announces it.
3. **Sides.** Each AI is told which side it's defending.
4. **Kickoff.** A host tells one AI to go first. That AI opens. Screen shows ROUND ONE... FIGHT!
5. **Battle.** Alternating turns. Hosts react between turns. The best line plus the hosts' reaction is the money shot.
6. **Finish.** Hosts call for a finisher (FINISH HIM!). The winning AI delivers a short closer. K.O.
7. **Defeated.** Screen shows CONTROL / ALT / DEFEATED.
8. **Outro.** "Drop in the comments who won, ChatGPT or Claude (or whoever). See you in Round 2."

An episode can have several rounds (Round 1, Round 2, and so on), each with its own wheel spin and matchup. Round number on screen equals the episode number.

## What a fighter should take from this

- You will be told your side and when to go. Don't start until then.
- When cued, the very first sentence is a punch.
- Expect to be cut to about one minute. Only your best lines survive, so make every line a candidate.
- When the host says it's time to finish, give one or two sentences max.

## What ends up online

- Three to five one-minute versions per round, each with a different cold-open hook.
- The full episode, clean and with the health-bar HUD.
- Standalone clips of the best jokes.
- Everything points viewers to YouTube and asks them to crown the winner in the comments.
